# Tollbound spine: the game in the hand

Priority: one thumb in a crowd. Four survivors lock and carry the rest: harm sorted by posture (thumbs-01), the dead walking your path (thumbs-55), lost life owed to a place (thumbs-02, changed) and crossing your own path (thumbs-07 changed, thumbs-27). Everything reads the same postures: still, moving, near, timed.

## 1. The game's words

## 2. The combat model

**Kinds of harm.** Every voice and every attack deals one kind.
- **Blow**: one hit: a strike, a swing, a thrown thing. An enemy Blow shows as its striking shape rising for its wind-up, 0.3 to 0.8 s.
- **Drag**: harm counted 4 times a second, without warning: a pressing crowd, riders, bad ground, a lingering voice.
- **Knell**: a heavy strike drawn on the ground as a shape that fills until it lands, 0.8 to 3 s. It ignores every modifier of its target except Wrong-footed.

**Protections: what the feet are doing.**
- **Brace**, against Blows: stand still 0.4 s and Blows taken are halved until the first step.
- **Stride**, against Drag: move at 70% of top speed or more and Drag taken is cut by 60%.
- **Lee**, against Knells: a spreading Knell, drawn outward from a source, spares a body with a prop or a large body (elite, keeper, heavy dead, burgess) on the line to the source within 3 m of it. A falling Knell, a circle darkening underfoot, has no lee: step out.

Standing in a crowd braces you against its Blows and gives you to its Drag; no posture covers all three.

**Wrong-footed.** Any body, living or dead, whose heading changed more than 90° within the last 0.5 s takes 50% more from all harm.

**How the dead move.** The common dead steer at the place you stood 1.2 s ago. Walking draws them into a train on your exact path; they turn where you turned, 1.2 s later, Wrong-footed at every corner you cut. Stop, and they reach you in 1.2 s.

**Conditions.**
1. **Wrong-footed.** As above; ends 0.5 s after the turn (keepers 0.25 s).
2. **In Procession.** Applied: a dead stays within 2.5 m of your wake for 5 s. Does: +15% speed, +30% harm taken, +25% experience. Ends: it touches you, is shoved, or strays 3 m off the wake. Interacts: the count (cap 40) is a stat voices can read.
3. **Moored.** Applied: when your path crosses your wake of the last 5 s, a stake is driven at the crossing and the dead within 2.5 m, up to 12, are tied to it in the order they followed. Does: each moves only within 2.5 m of the stake; felling or shoving one jerks the next 1 m inward and Wrong-foots it. Ends: after 4 s or below 2 bodies; one stake per 3 s, no body twice in 6 s. Interacts: the moored stand, so they Brace and take Drag in full.
4. **Sounded.** Applied: keep a large dead 4 to 6 m away for 2 s. Does, for 6 s: harm against it rolls its top value and its attacks show 0.3 s earlier. Ends: 0.5 s outside the band.
5. **Coiled.** Applied: each full sunwise circuit you walk round a large dead within 6 m adds a Coil (max 3). Does: its attacks come 12% slower per Coil; one widdershins circuit spends them all, each a Blow of 150% of your strongest voice hit. Ends: 10 s after the last circuit. Interacts: circling at 4 to 6 m also Sounds it.
6. **Mourning.** Applied: when an elite or keeper dies, to common dead within 8 m. Does: 6 s full (black, faces the body, will not strike), then 6 s half (grey, half speed, half the strikes). Ends: on time; harm in full mourning ends it and the body strikes 30% faster for 6 s. Interacts: mourners stand, so Blows do half and Drag wakes them; walk away and they sit out 12 s.
7. **Clung.** Applied: hangers-on, small dead, leap onto you from 1.5 m. Does: per rider (max 5), 8% slower and one Drag a second; standing still lets another within 3 m climb on every 2 s. Ends: never by time; pass within 0.8 m of a large dead at Stride speed and every rider moves onto it. Interacts: riders pull you below Stride, so their Drag lands in full.
8. **Coined.** Applied: by voices, knots and favours that say so, for 5 s. Does: it walks straight on, turning only at props, striking whatever it walks into, the dead included. Ends: on time, or a Knell within 4 m; it drops two coins, four if laid while coined. Interacts: it leaves the wake, so coin the head of a train and step aside.

**Healing: wounds owed to a place.** Harm that lands on you opens a wound on the ground where you stood, holding the life it took; harm within 2 m of an open wound joins the newest one, and at most 10 are open. A wound's depth is the warning its harm was shown for: Drag 0, a Blow its wind-up, a Knell its fill; a joined wound keeps the deepest. Stand inside a wound (1 m) and after 0.4 s it gives its life back evenly over 1 s + 2 × depth; stepping off keeps what came back. A wound open for 60 s scars: what is left comes off greatest life until the vigil ends. Nothing else heals unless a voice, knot, favour or vow says so; scars stay at the lychgate.

**Heed and vows.** An attack locks its aim when first shown. Heed, 100 per character, that no vow holds shows every Knell earlier, up to 1 s at 100 free; Ear, the Register, Sounded and lit lamps add, never past 1 s in all. A vow holds Heed for a promise the thumb keeps or breaks; broken, it pays nothing until the next toll. Stability (30: stay within 12 m of where you stood at the last toll; +25% harm); the Unbroken Round (35: touch the bell-post every toll period; +5% harm and +2% speed per period kept, to 8); Alms (25: leave each period's first find lying; +5% experience per unclaimed cairn).

**The two extra buttons.** Button one is the class verb, shown only where one exists: the Toll-keeper's post and the Backward-ringer's alarm. Button two, every class, is **Cast Away**: your newest find flies 6 m ahead, the common dead within 10 m crowd it for 5 s, and it is destroyed.

**The dead and you.** Both obey every rule above. Only you wound and mend; you carry only Wrong-footed and Clung; keepers cannot be Moored, Coined or put in mourning; your attributes sharpen your postures, theirs never change.

**What the screen shows.**
- Under every body its posture: a closed ring braced, a streak striding, a white flash wrong-footed, so the crowd's marks say which voices will bite. Conditions are shape and colour, never icons.
- On the ground: Knells filling, with their lee cut out, or darkening as circles; your wake and any mooring stake; wounds as red discs, darker when deeper, greying toward a scar.
- Heed as a bar under life; the toll as a ring closing round the screen edge in its last 3 s.

**The model in four sentences.**
1. There are three kinds of harm: a Blow is one hit, Drag keeps biting while you are in a crowd or on bad ground, and a Knell is a great strike drawn on the ground before it lands.
2. Stand still to brace against Blows, run hard to shake off Drag, and put a pillar or a big body between you and a Knell; the dead are protected the same way, so hit the running and grind the standing.
3. Anyone who has just turned sharply is Wrong-footed and takes half as much again, and the dead walk the path you walked, so they turn wherever you turned.
4. The life you lose stays on the ground where you lost it: stand on it to take it back before it scars.

## 3. Attributes

One for each thing the thumb does: stop, go, close in, wait for the moment.
- **Knee**: +2 greatest life; +1% to effects tagged Still. Every 10: Brace takes hold 0.03 s sooner (least 0.1 s) and cuts Blows 2 points more (at most 70%).
- **Heel**: +0.4% top speed; +1% to effects tagged Moving. Every 10: Stride holds at 2 points less of top speed (least 50%).
- **Hand**: +1% to effects on dead within 3 m; +1% pickup reach. Every 10: wounds mend from 0.1 m farther (at most 2 m).
- **Ear**: +1% to effects tagged Timed (voices that speak on the quarters, the toll or an appointment). Every 10: Knells show 0.05 s sooner, within the 1 s limit.

Every voice and item names one attribute and a number wanted. Nothing is locked: below the number, all it gives works at your value divided by the number. A voice wants its tag's attribute, 10 per tier (1 to 5); an item wants that of the posture that wrote most of its lines, 4 per line. A level gives 2 points to the class's first attribute and 1 to its second; tree path nodes give points of your choice.

## 4. Classes and methods

**Ringer** (Hand, Knee). A tower ringer who kept hold of the rope. The thumb rocks back and forth inside the crowd.
- Starts with Tenor Stroke: a Blow ring 2.5 m round the Ringer every 3 s.
- Rule, the pull: a reversal of more than 120° within 0.3 s makes the voice in the first place speak at once (at most once per 0.4 s) and does not Wrong-foot the Ringer. Standing still 1 s sets the bell: the next pull speaks double.
- Stander: while set, each dead within 2 m leans on the stay with its weight (common 1, large 3, elite 5), a bar rising toward 12. The next pull makes every voice speak once in order, 0.1 s apart, each +8% per weight leaning; past 12 the stay breaks and nothing speaks for 3 s.
- Backward-ringer: button one rings backward: a mark is set where the Ringer stands, every common dead in the district walks to it instead of the wake, and each second backward brings the next toll 1 s nearer. Pressed again, the gathered crowd turns for the Ringer at once.

**Verger** (Heel, Hand). Cleared the road before processions with the verge. The thumb drives straight through the thickest crowd.
- Starts with the Verge: every 0.9 s it raps the two dead nearest the wedge's edges (Blow).
- Rule, the wedge: moving at half top speed or more and not Wrong-footed, the Verger opens a wedge 3 m long and 120° wide ahead; the dead in it are shouldered 1.5 m aside and cannot strike for 0.5 s. Behind and beside are bare; the parted dead turn about for the wake and are Wrong-footed behind him.
- Beadle: the parted dead stand 5 s in two still files facing inward along the lane just walked, unable to strike, and Bracing; if the Verger turns back into the lane, both files strike inward at once.
- Marshal: while the Verger stands still, every dead in procession halts and stands, up to 4 s, not again for 6 s; the rest keep coming.

**Toll-keeper** (Hand, Heel). Kept the bar at bridge and gate. The thumb circles a post, sweeping the following train like a clock hand.
- Starts with Pike: a 4 m thrust at the nearest dead every 1.5 s (Blow).
- Rule, the bar: button one plants the toll-post. Within 7 m of it a bar joins post and keeper, and every dead it passes over pays toll: a Blow that doubles each time the same body pays again within 10 s, at most 8 times.
- Parish Clerk: at each toll the kind of dead laid most since the last toll is struck off the roll: none of it rise in the next interval, and every other kind rises 15% more.
- Alderman: a dead that pays three times within 10 s and still stands becomes a burgess: it stops, cannot harm or be harmed, blocks like stone, gives lee, and adds 1 coin to your finds at each toll.

**Tuner** (Ear, Heel). A bell-foundry's tuner. The thumb sets the note by how far it pushes.
- Starts with Fork: a Blow ring, 6 m every 2.4 s at the lowest note, 2 m every 0.6 s at the highest.
- Rule, pace is pitch: push depth sets the note of every voice in five steps. Under 40% push (tenor) voices wait 2.5 times as long, reach twice as far and strike 2.2 times as hard; over 85% (treble) they wait 0.4 as long, reach half as far and strike at 0.55; steps between scale evenly; standing still holds the note. Treble Strides; tenor neither Braces nor Strides.
- Gallery-walker: a voice cast within 2 m of a wall does not spread; it runs both ways along the wall for 20 m at 10 m/s, striking once every body within 1.5 m of it.
- Deep-caller: standing still 1 s sets every voice's reach to the distance of the nearest dead (2 to 8 m), drawn as a chalk ring, until the next such stop; notes then change only pace and weight.

**Lamplighter** (Ear, Heel). Walked the city's round of lamps. The thumb plans a circuit of fixed posts and keeps it against the clock.
- Starts with Taper: every 2 s each lit lamp throws a spark (Blow) at the dead nearest it in its light.
- Rule, the round: six lamp-posts stand in every district. Passing within 2 m lights one for 45 s; every toll puts all out. Each lit lamp makes your voices speak 10% faster, and Knells in its 5 m light show 0.4 s sooner.
- Snuffer: passing a lit lamp again snuffs it: a Knell fills its light in 0.5 s, its harm the seconds burned times a base, and the dead it strikes walk on their last heading for 3 s.
- Remembrancer: each lamp keeps up to 6 casts your voices made in its light on your last visit and repeats them at half harm while it burns.

**Organ-blower** (Knee, Heel). Pumped the cathedral organ's bellows. The thumb runs long, then stands short.
- Starts with Pipe: a Blow cone 6 m long and 40° wide toward where it last walked.
- Rule, wind: voices never speak while it moves. Walking fills the wind-chest 1 unit per metre, to 30, shown as a weight on a string over the figure; standing still lets it out, voices speaking at three times their rate for 10 units a second until empty.
- Treader: standing, the crowd's press is wind: every 5 dead within 3 m add 1 unit each half second. On the first step the wind left runs out through the spot as a race 2 m wide and up to 10 m long, away from the densest side, dealing Drag by the wind it carries.
- Organist: standing, each voice holds its note until 5 dead stand inside what it would strike, or 4 s pass; each full second held adds 20% harm and spends no wind.

## 5. The world

**Pitch.** Tollbound was a cathedral-city on a tidal river, and its five great bells kept its dead asleep. The river rose, the towers flooded, the bells fell dumb, and the dead rose in every parish. You keep vigil in the drowned streets. Every 75 seconds the dumb bell is struck and more dead rise; at the eighth stroke its keeper comes. Lay the keeper and the bell speaks again. Leave by a lychgate while you can still carry what you found.

**Tone.** (1) The dead are the city's own people, not monsters: each still works its trade, and its harm is what that trade did. (2) Every light has a source you can see; dark means unlit, never evil. (3) Plain English from trades, rites, bells and the river, and a shape where a number would do.

**Naming.** Places are "the" and what was done there (the Dial). The dead are named for a trade or habit (the Searcher, the Link-boy, Left-turner, hangers-on), never a creature word. Keepers take their bell's or office's name (the Lock-keeper), bells a cast name (Great Hour), voices a sound or tool (Fork). No invented syllables, apostrophes or Latin.

**The districts, in the order met.** All hold standing water of known depth: knee-deep slows everyone 15%, waist-deep 35%, which takes Stride away.
1. **The Dial.** A round plaza 70 m across: slab floor, cylinder bell-post with a cone bell, eight box stones on the radii, low ring walls; chalk, noon gold, slate violet, bronze, moss. A 90° wedge of shadow sweeps sunwise once per toll, which rings as it crosses the north stone; in it the dead are 40% faster and Knells show 0.3 s later. Bell: Great Hour. Keeper: the Half-muffled Keeper, every stroke a Knell landing twice on one ground. Phase 1: the stroke, then a 30% echo 2 s later. Phase 2 (60%): the echo first as the only warning, the stroke 1.2 s after. Phase 3 (25%): three echoes, then their strokes in reverse order.
2. **The Ropewalk.** Lanes 6 m wide and 60 m long between box sheds, cross-alleys every 20 m, rope-rack posts; tar black, hemp tan, wet slate, rust red, lamp yellow. The dead come only from lane ends, so every moment asks which end to face; each toll shuts two cross-alleys and opens two. Bell: Long Tom, carried by its dead. Keeper: the Flogged Bell. Phase 1: it charges down a lane shown 1 s ahead, shedding four dead at each wall. Phase 2 (60%): every 6 s, after a 1.5 s swell, a Knell fills the arena whose only lee is behind large dead, so the player spares them. Phase 3 (25%): one ring per character level, then it lies open 8 s.
3. **The Founders' Sink.** Soot ground, loam furnaces (tapered boxes), cylinder chimneys, cone slag heaps, casting pits 4 m wide; soot, loam, ember orange, bronze, verdigris. A Blow landing in a pit is answered 1 s later by a 4 m Knell of the same harm on living and dead alike; a pit answered more than 3 times in 5 s cracks. Bell: the Maiden, never poured. Keeper: Keeper of the Unpoured Bell. Phase 1: a wax figure of eight faces records the harm each takes and drips wax circles (falling Knells) that set into pillars. Phase 2 (50%): bronze takes its place; the most harmed faces take double, untouched ones a fifth, and it turns its thin faces away at 30° a second behind a 4 m swing shown 1 s ahead.
4. **The Strand.** Mudflats under river stairs (stepped boxes), mooring posts, three stranded hulks; mud brown, tin-grey water, bone, weed green, buoy red. The toll is the tide: odd tolls flood, even tolls ebb, by twelfths through each interval, slack 3 s at the toll. The ebb bares 30 m of silt (30% slower off the stairs); the flood floats the hulks to ground somewhere new. Bell: Low Water. Keeper: the Lock-keeper, in a lock 24 m long. Phase 1: the water rises a band every 15 s as he heaves ballast stones (falling Knells). Phase 2: a race runs down one of three lanes, streaking 1.5 s first, pressing what it carries against the far gate (Drag). Phase 3: a bore crosses every 12 s; the only lee is within 3 m behind him.
5. **The Hanging Quarter.** The cathedral's ringing chamber: oak frames, lead floors, forty ropes with striped sallies (cylinders); oak brown, lead grey, wool red, white, wool blue. Each rope runs to a floor circle 5 to 10 m away; anything walking through a sally pulls it, and 1 s later a 2 m falling Knell lands on its circle. Bell: the Tenor. Keeper: the Conductor of the Dead Band: six bells round the arena, each tilting 0.6 s before a 40° wedge (Knell), in a row shown by numbered lamps. Phase 1: rounds, 1 to 6. Phase 2 (70%): row to row, a bell keeps its place or swaps with a neighbour. Phase 3 (30%): all six at once after 2 s, but a bell earlier struck by Blows worth 5% of his life is cracked and silent.

**The frame in the fiction.** The hub is the Lantern, the glass stage atop the cathedral tower. The drowned tower clock still strikes each dumb bell every 75 s, and a dumb bell wakes the dead instead of calming them; at the eighth stroke its dead keeper comes to see who rings. A lychgate opens for 25 s after each elite or keeper falls and at every third toll; standing in it rings you down, six strokes a second apart, each banking a sixth of your finds in your order and silencing a sixth of your voices; driven out, you keep what went through. At no life you are Tolled-for: voices stop, you run 20% faster, and a lychgate opens at the far edge; reach it before the next toll and live without your finds, or join the dead. Every toll leaves Hum in those who hear it, caught at once by the Bellframe below the Lantern, where the dead are recast as bells. With all five keepers laid, the districts join into one open city.

## 6. Progression

**The City Plan, the shared tree.** The old street plan: eight ring roads round the cathedral close, twelve straight streets out, about 600 nodes, six class doors on the first ring. Points lay one unbranching walk from your door, extending only its head; refunds come off the head. Stepping back onto your route costs a point, doubles that node once and closes a loop: up to 12 nodes inside work at half strength, free. A node on ring N works only from the Nth toll of a vigil; outer rings are strongest. Straight Mile nodes gain 25% per held node past the fourth in their straight street, to 10. Planning is drawing a walk, as play is: where to cross yourself to fence a rich block, which straight to hold, and how far out to go, which is how long you mean to stay.

**Methods and favours.** At level 15 a character takes a method; each peal, a vigil with one rule (keep 20 in procession through a toll), gives 2 of its 8 points. Levelling never pauses: three favours rise as shapes 4 m away; walk into one before they sink in 8 s; an untaken level adds a fourth. Plain favours add numbers; turned ones change a rule, as Countermarch: a U-turn passes through the dead and turns them.

**Items.** A base with one fixed line, found blank. Each toll survived while it is carried unbanked writes a line, at most 8, from what you did most that period: stood, strode, kept within 3 m of the dead, or, if the toll found you at a landmark, Timed. The line is drawn from that posture's table, weighted by base, sized in a band rising with the toll, and works only while you do what wrote it. No tiers: worth is notches. Banked, it is fixed; carried at death, lost; worn gear returns to the stash. To change one, strike a line and wear it into a vigil: the first toll survived rewrites it; dying erases it and both neighbours. Or cut a line for good: the rest rise 10%.

**The Bellframe.** The Nth toll pays N Hum times the landmarks you have stood beside at a toll this vigil, kept at once; a death by a new cause pays 40 Hum times its toll. Hum buys places, 100 rising 50% each, to 8. Each dead character becomes a bell weighing its last vigil's tolls, holding a boon from its most-used posture; each new character rings bells up to the places bought. In weight order, neighbours 10 to 30% apart ring true, +5% to every boon per pair, so short lives count. The Register shows each attack that killed a character 0.15 s earlier to all later ones, up to three deaths.

**Two ways to play.** Household: standing characters until death; Bills, a district with 1 to 4 causes (water a band deeper), are its endgame, and bequests unlock classes and Bills. Watch-night: five districts in one sitting from nothing; at the second toll the two class acts you did most rise as dead tradespeople; stand by one 2 s to take its class, and the other hunts you.
