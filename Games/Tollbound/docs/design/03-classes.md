# Tollbound: classes and callings

Every class is one thing a thumb can do in a crowd: reverse, drive straight through, circle a post, walk a round of fixed places, run and then stand, or stop early and dig in. Each favours a different pair of the four attributes, and each attribute is favoured by three classes. The words are those of `01-world.md`; the stats named in backticks are in section 15.8 of `02-combat.md`.

A character starts with 10 points in its class's first attribute and 5 in its second, and each level adds 2 and 1. Every number in a class rule is a stat, so the Plan, finds and knots can move it.

## How each class plays in the hand

**Change-ringer.** The thumb rocks. In the middle of a crowd you flick back and forth, and every flick is a pull that fires your first voice. The common dead aim at where you were 1.2 s ago, so each flick makes the crowd turn where you turned, Wrong-footed for half a second, just as your Tenor Stroke lands. A pause is not wasted: a second of stillness sets the bell and doubles the next pull, and standing braces you against the crowd's Blows while its Drag bites. The builder decides what speaks first, because the pull fires only the voice in your first place. The class leans on Hand and Knee, on `pull-window` and `set-time`, and on anything that rewards the Wrong-footed.

**Verger.** The thumb drives straight. Where other classes go round a crowd, the Verger goes through it, and the crowd parts to either side of the wedge, turned away. You must keep moving fast and must not cut corners: a sharp turn wrong-foots you, and the wedge closes in the thick of the crowd. Behind you the parted dead turn back for your wake and are Wrong-footed there, so voices that reach behind you, or lie along your path, feed on them. Striding all the time means the crowd's Drag hardly bites, but Blows land in full, so the Verger's danger is the striker it drives into. Turn About sends a long train back down its own path, a crowd trampling itself. The class leans on Heel and Hand and on `wedge-length`.

**Toll-gatherer.** The thumb circles a post. You plant the post where you want the fight, and the dead following you round it are swept by the bar like a clock hand. A body that pays again within ten seconds pays double, so tight fast circles with a long train make the bar terrible, and a break in the rhythm lets every body's toll fall back. Where to plant the post, between which walls and beside which crowd, is the class's one big decision, made again whenever the fight moves. Circling keeps you Striding, and the train following you round is In Procession, ripe for the bar. The class leans on Ear (keeping the rhythm) and Hand, and on `bar-length`.

**Lamp-trimmer.** The thumb plans a round. Six lamps stand in every district; you walk the circuit and keep them lit against the clock, and every toll puts them all out. Lit lamps make your voices quicker and throw sparks at the dead who walk past them, and their light shows Swells sooner, so a fight held inside your lamplight is a safer one. Each district sets the round differently, so the class plays differently in each, and the Long Pole lights a lamp across a crowd you will not walk through. The class leans on Ear (the clock) and Heel (the round), and on `lamp-burn`.

**Organ-blower.** The thumb runs long and then stands short. Nothing speaks while you move, so you run thirty metres through the district to fill the chest, stop in the right place, and let three seconds of voices out at three times their rate. Running, you Stride, and the crowd's Drag hardly bites; standing, you Brace against Blows, and your Pipe is Drag, which bites in full the standing crowd that presses round you. Gust is the way out of a stop made in the wrong place: all the wind becomes a shove that clears the ring round you and breaks the dead's Brace. The class leans on Knee and Heel and on `chest-size`.

**Sexton.** The thumb stops early. A grave is only as deep as the seconds you spent digging before the crowd arrived; once you are in it you are below the crowd's press, no Drag reaches you, and the dead who walk into the pit are laid in it. But every place you dug is half a second of climbing out, unbraced, and a full grave leaves you on its mound in the middle of whatever is left. The Sexton is a bet on how deep to go and when to leave. Close is the quick way out, and its mound is cover from the next Swell. The class leans on Knee (standing) and Ear (timing the climb), and on `dig-rate`.

## The twelve callings

| Id | Class | Name | The rule it adds |
|---|---|---|---|
| `stander` | change-ringer | Stander | While the bell is set, each dead within 2 m leans on the stay with its weight (small 1, large 3, elite 5), shown as a bar rising toward 12. The next pull makes every voice speak once, in order, 0.1 s apart, each 8% stronger for every point of weight leaning. Past 12 the stay breaks, and nothing speaks for 3 s. |
| `backward-ringer` | change-ringer | Backward-ringer | The trade becomes **Ring Backward**: a mark is set where you stand, and every common dead in the district steers to the mark instead of your wake; each second you ring backward brings the next toll 1 s nearer. Press again, or wait 10 s, and the whole gathered crowd turns for you at once. Recharge 20 s. |
| `beadle` | verger | Beadle | The dead your wedge parts no longer turn back: they stand for 5 s in two still files facing inward along the lane you have just walked (the condition `filed`). They do not strike, and they are Braced. If you turn back into the lane, both files strike inward at once. |
| `wand-bearer` | verger | Wand-bearer | When you stand still, every dead In Procession halts where it is for as long as you stand, up to 4 s, and cannot be halted again for 6 s; the rest keep coming. A halted train stands: Braced, and open to Drag in full. |
| `registrar` | toll-gatherer | Registrar | At each toll the kind of dead you laid most since the last toll is struck off the roll: none of that kind rises in the next interval, and every other kind rises 15% more. The roll fills at the screen's edge as you lay them. |
| `alderman` | toll-gatherer | Alderman | A dead that pays at your bar three times within 10 s and still stands becomes a burgess (the condition `burgess`): it stops, cannot harm or be harmed, blocks like stone, gives lee, and pays 1 coin into your haul at each toll it stands. |
| `snuffer` | lamp-trimmer | Snuffer | Passing a lit lamp again, 3 s or more after lighting it, snuffs it: a Swell spreads through its 5 m light in 0.5 s, its harm the seconds the lamp burned times a base, and the dead it strikes walk blind on their last heading for 3 s. |
| `recorder` | lamp-trimmer | Recorder | Each lamp keeps up to six casts that your voices made in its light on your last visit, and repeats them in order, at half harm, for as long as it burns. |
| `treader` | organ-blower | Treader | Standing, the crowd's press is wind: every 5 dead within 3 m add 1 unit each half second. On your first step the wind left runs out through the spot as a race 2 m wide and up to 10 m long, away from the densest side, dealing Drag by the wind it carries. |
| `organist` | organ-blower | Organist | Standing, each voice holds its note until 5 dead stand inside what it would strike, or 4 s pass; each full second held adds 20% harm and spends no wind. |
| `banksman` | sexton | Banksman | Every grave throws up a spoil bank, a wall of earth 3 m long on the far side of the grave from where you walked in. The dead cannot cross a bank and are turned round its ends toward the pit. A bank lasts until its grave is full. |
| `surety` | sexton | Surety | Standing 0.5 s on the place where one of the dead fell, within 3 s of its fall, takes on its debt: double experience from it, and one debt, each +1% harm and −0.5% top speed, without limit. Debts are paid off only by ringing down at a lychgate, each becoming 1 Hum. |

### What each calling rewards

- **Stander:** letting the crowd pile on while Braced, and judging the limit before the stay breaks.
- **Backward-ringer:** gathering a district into one heap for one great pull, paid for with the clock.
- **Beadle:** long straight drives that lay corridors of standing dead for Drag and line voices.
- **Wand-bearer:** switching the train between runners (for Blows) and standers (for Drag) at will.
- **Registrar:** choosing, by what you lay, which part of the horde the district will lack for the next 75 seconds.
- **Alderman:** a bar tuned weak on purpose, and streets filled with tenants who are walls, lee and coin.
- **Snuffer:** choosing at every lamp between keeping its upkeep and cashing in its age.
- **Recorder:** staging fights in lamplight, composing the district by which voice spoke where.
- **Treader:** letting the crowd press in, and aiming the release by which side you leave.
- **Organist:** drawing the dead into a knot and holding them there until it is worth the note.
- **Banksman:** choosing each stop for the pen its bank completes as well as for the pit.
- **Surety:** greed paced by your own growing slowness, and knowing which body is the last one worth taking.

## Taking a calling, and opening classes

- In the Household a character takes its calling after it first lays the Ropewalk's keeper. A calling is its rule plus a small tree of 8 points; each **peal**, a vigil kept to one rule of that calling from the first toll to the keeper's fall (for a Stander, "keep the bell set through a toll with 12 weight leaning"), gives 2 of them.
- In the Watch-night the calling is courted in the second district, as the class is in the first (`04-progression.md`).
- The Change-ringer, the Verger and the Organ-blower are open from the start. The Toll-gatherer, the Lamp-trimmer and the Sexton each open with a bequest: one found in the Dial, one in the Ropewalk, one in the Founders' Sink.

## Where the two spines disagreed

- **The six:** the Change-ringer (both spines; renamed from Ringer, which is an enemy in a well-known game), the Verger, the Toll-gatherer, the Lamp-trimmer and the Organ-blower (hands), and the Sexton (depth). The Toll-keeper became the Toll-gatherer and the Lamplighter the Lamp-trimmer, because "Keeper's Toll" and "The Lamplighters League" are games.
- **The Tuner leaves,** though both spines had it, because pace set by how far the stick is pushed does not exist on a keyboard, and a class one of the two platforms cannot play is not a class.
- **The Leadsman, the Warper and the Chandler leave:** holding a fixed distance from a crowd that follows you is what walking away already does; a class that never walks would need its own version of every movement rule; coats lost in place of life are a stock shield, and the Lamp-trimmer already walks out-and-back rounds.
- **The Sexton joins the hands spine's five,** because standing to dig in is the one thumb act none of them asks for, and its grave is the posture model's answer to standing still in a crowd.
- **Every class has a trade (depth),** because the second thumb should have something of its own in every class.
- **The attributes are spread** so that each class favours a different pair and each attribute three classes, where the hands spine gave five of six classes Heel.
- **Callings:** the Change-ringer keeps the Stander (both) and takes the Backward-ringer (hands) over the Half-muffler (depth), because the half-muffler needed Ring and Crack, which left with weight matching. The Sexton keeps the depth spine's two, renamed: Fossor is Latin, and Sin-eater is an enemy faction of a well-known game, so its sins became debts. The Marshal became the Wand-bearer (a tabletop class has the name), the Parish Clerk the Registrar (a post in another game), and the Remembrancer the Recorder (an order in a well-known tabletop game).
