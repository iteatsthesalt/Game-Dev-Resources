# Tollbound: the world

This file holds the game's words, its pitch and tone, how things are named, the five districts and their keepers, the fiction of the frame, and the direction for art and sound. The combat model is in `02-combat.md`, the classes in `03-classes.md`, and progression in `04-progression.md`. Every idea here was invented for this game from the pool in `pool/`; see `README.md`.

Hush, draught and the dodge roll have no successor: the game has no shield, no healing drink and no dodge.

## The pitch

Tollbound was a cathedral-city on a tidal river. Its five great bells were rung at every turn of the tide, and their ringing kept the city's dead asleep. Then the river rose, the towers flooded and the bells fell dumb. The drowned tower clock still swings each bell every seventy-five seconds, but a dumb bell cannot calm anyone: every silent stroke wakes more of the dead, and at the eighth stroke the district's keeper comes up to see who is ringing.

You are one of the last of the living, kept in the Lantern at the top of the cathedral tower. You go down into one district at a time and keep vigil there. You steer with one thumb through hundreds of the dead while your voices speak by themselves. Lay the keeper and the district's bell speaks again. Leave by a lychgate while you can still carry what you found. You have one life; when you lose it, the Bellframe recasts you as a bell, and the next of the living rings it.

## Tone

1. The dead are the city's own people, not monsters. Each still works its trade or keeps its custom, and its harm is what that trade did. There are no villains and no gore.
2. Every light has a source you can see. Dark means unlit, never evil.
3. Silence is the danger and sound is the remedy. The bells are dumb, the dead answer to sound, and a laid keeper gives a bell its voice back.
4. Plain English from trades, rites, bells and the river, and a shape on the screen wherever a number would do.

## Naming

- **Places** are "the" and what was done there: the Dial, the Ropewalk, the Founders' Sink, the Foreshore, the Hanging Quarter.
- **Bells** have a cast name: Great Hour, the Flogged Bell, the Unpoured Bell, Low Water, the Tenor.
- **The dead** are named for a trade, a custom, or how they were buried: Wake-walkers, hangers-on, the Searcher, the Link-boy, the Coin-eyed, the Mortsafe, Bell-bearers, the Layer.
- **Keepers** take their bell's or their office's name: the Half-muffled Keeper, the Lock-keeper, the Ringing Master.
- **Voices** are a sound or a tool: Tenor Stroke, the Verge, the Bar, Taper, Pipe, Spadeful.
- **Knots** are rope or ringing words: Slip-knot, Running Line, Back-splice, Open Lead.
- **Favours** are a mourner's gift or a household custom: Funeral Biscuit, Pall on Hire, Tithe.
- **Observances** are named for the custom kept: Stability, the Unbroken Round, Alms, Telling the Bees.
- **Never:** invented syllables, apostrophes inside invented words, Latin, or a creature word (no skeleton, zombie, ghoul, wraith, vampire, demon, cultist and the like); no "Vow of" names; no "Knell" (it is another game's title) and no "Keeper's Toll" (likewise); nothing on `denylist.txt`.
- **Ids** are lower-case words joined by dashes: `change-ringer`, `founders-sink`, `wrong-footed`, `life-max`.

### The keepers' fights

**The Half-muffled Keeper (the Dial).** Leather is strapped to one side of its clapper, so every stroke it makes lands twice on the same ground. In the first phase each attack is a full Swell followed 2 s later by an echo at 30% on the same ground, so ground it has struck stays dangerous. At 60% of its life the leather is on the other side: the faint echo comes first and is the only warning, and the full stroke lands on the same ground 1.2 s later. At 25% it is rung backward, the old alarm: three echoes are laid one after another, then their three full strokes land in reverse order, the last laid first. The fight teaches the player to read the ground and the order in which it was marked.

**The Flogged Bell (the Ropewalk).** A great bell that was flogged and had its tongue torn out, carried through the lanes by its dead. In the first phase it is tongueless and charges down a lane along a line shown 1 s ahead, and every wall it strikes sheds four of its bearers into the fight. At 60% it is re-tongued: every 6 s, after a 1.5 s swell of sound, a Swell fills the whole arena, and the only lee is behind large dead, so the player must spare the big dead and stand behind them. At 25% it tells the player's years, one stroke for each character level, each a falling Swell 2 m across drawn under the player 0.8 s before it lands; then it lies spent for 8 s.

**The Keeper of the Unpoured Bell (the Founders' Sink).** The bell was never poured, and its keeper is the wax model of it. In the first phase it has eight faces, and each records the harm it takes; it drips wax in falling Swells (shown 1 s) that set into pillars, so the arena fills with cover. At 50% the wax runs out and bronze takes its exact place: the faces most harmed in wax are thin and take double, faces never harmed take a fifth, and it turns its thin faces away from the player at 30° a second behind a 4 m swinging Blow shown 1 s ahead. The player sculpts the flaw in wax and then circles to reach it in bronze.

**The Lock-keeper (the Foreshore).** Fought in a lock chamber 24 m long. In the first phase the chamber fills: the water rises one band every 15 s while he heaves ballast stones, each a falling Swell shown 1 s. In the second phase the water stands at the waist, where nobody can Stride; he opens paddles and a race runs down one of three lanes, its water streaking 1.5 s first, and anything in it takes Drag and is swept to the far gate and pressed there. In the third phase the gates open and a bore crosses the chamber every 12 s as a spreading Swell from the gate; the only lee is the 3 m behind the keeper himself, inside his reach.

**The Ringing Master (the Hanging Quarter).** Six bells hang round the arena. Each tilts mouth-up 0.6 s before it sends a 40° wedge, a spreading Swell from the bell, and they speak in an order shown by six numbered lamps. In the first phase they ring rounds, 1 to 6. At 70% they ring changes: from one row to the next a bell may only keep its place or swap with a neighbour, so each row can be read from the last. At 30% they fire: all six speak at once after 2 s, except bells the player struck earlier with Blows worth 5% of the Master's life, which are cracked and silent and leave safe wedges.

## The frame in the fiction

**The Lantern.** The glass stage at the top of the cathedral tower is the one lit place left above the water. The living keep it, and every character sets out from it and comes back to it. Here the player changes finds, ties knots, walks the Plan and visits the Bellframe.

**Tolls.** The drowned tower clock still swings each district's dumb bell every 75 seconds. A dumb stroke wakes the dead instead of calming them, so each toll raises the danger, and each brings up more of what the dead were buried with, so it raises the rewards. At the eighth toll the keeper comes. When the keeper is laid, the bell speaks: from then on that district's tolls ring true, its keeper's Bill can be found, and the vigil goes on, as dangerous as ever, for as long as the player dares.

**Lychgates.** A lychgate opens for 25 seconds after each elite or keeper falls and at every third toll. Standing in it rings the character down, six strokes a second apart: each stroke banks a sixth of the haul, in the order the player set, and silences a sixth of the character's voices. The sixth stroke ends the vigil. Driven out sooner, the character keeps what was banked, stays in the vigil, and its silenced voices come back one every 2 seconds.

**One life, and Tolled-for.** At no life a character is not yet dead but Tolled-for, as the dying were: its voices stop, it runs faster, and a lychgate opens at the far edge of the district. If it gets there before the next toll it lives and loses its whole haul. Otherwise it joins the dead. A dead character never returns. Its worn finds go to the Household's chest and its haul is lost.

**The Bellframe and Hum.** Below the Lantern hangs the Bellframe, where the founders recast each fallen character as a bell weighing the tolls it heard in its life. Every toll leaves Hum in those who hear it, and the Bellframe catches it at once, so a death, a closed game or a call that interrupts costs nothing already heard. Hum opens the Bellframe's places, and the bells of the dead give their gifts to everyone who comes after.

**The open city.** When all five bells speak, the districts join into one open city, walked on one life.

## Art direction

**The look.** Every figure and prop is built from about ten unit shapes (box, tapered box, cylinder, sphere, cone), drawn into a small target with real light and cast shadows, and scaled up with hard pixels. A person stands about 1.7 m and about 40 pixels tall. At that size a figure is read by three things only: its silhouette, the one long thing it carries, and the way it moves. Faces and fine detail do not exist.

**Shape language.**
- The living stand upright: a square box torso, a sphere head held high, and one long tool that breaks the silhouette. Each class is told apart by its tool alone.
- The dead slump: a tapered box torso tilted 15° forward, the head set low and forward, arms hanging. Their walk is a drag of the feet, not a stride.
- Large dead are twice as wide, with short legs. Width is how the player knows which bodies give lee.
- Bells and everything to do with bells are cones and cylinders. A keeper is two to three times a person's height and has the silhouette of its bell or office.
- Water is a flat plane with real reflection. Tide marks run round every pillar in bands, so depth can be read at a glance.

**Colour.**
- Each district has five colours: ground, walls, two accents, and one signal colour.
- The language of danger never changes between districts, so it is learned once. An enemy Blow's striking part flashes white at the top of its wind-up. A Swell fills its shape in pale bone with a dark rim, and its lee is cut out of the fill as clean ground. Wounds are deep red discs, darker when deeper, greying as they near a scar.
- Postures are marked under every body: a closed pale ring when Braced, a short trailing streak when Striding, a white flash of the outline when Wrong-footed.
- The dead wear their district's colours at 40% saturation, with one bright accent per family. The player's class colour is on its coat and on the life bar.

**What glows.** Only flames and hot metal: lamps and tapers, the small light every living character carries at the belt, the Link-boy's torch, the Wick-walkers' wicks, the rims of the casting pits and the metal in them, and the Lantern itself. Swells, wounds and conditions are drawn as shapes and colours; they never glow.

**How each class reads at 40 pixels.**
- **Change-ringer:** a bell-rope rising from its hands with a striped sally in red, white and blue; it rocks back and forth in place.
- **Verger:** an upright verge (a thin cylinder topped by a sphere) held before it and a black gown to the ground; it moves in long straight lines behind the wedge drawn ahead of it.
- **Toll-gatherer:** a broad flat hat and a pike across the shoulders; its striped toll-post stands nearby, and the bar is drawn between them.
- **Lamp-trimmer:** a long pole held high with a small glowing flame at its tip; lit lamps around the district mark its round.
- **Organ-blower:** a bellows of two hinged tapered boxes on its back, and a weight on a string over its head that sinks as the wind-chest fills.
- **Sexton:** a spade held low, a hunched dark coat; when it digs, the figure sinks into the pit drawn round it.

**How each family of the dead reads at 40 pixels.** (Each family's members and rules come later, from the pool, in the forms its notes allow.)
- **Wake-walkers,** the common dead: the slumped figure with nothing in its hands, always in trains.
- **Hangers-on:** half-height box figures that move in quick hops; once riding, they show as small boxes stuck to the player's figure.
- **The heavy dead:** the wide figures, slow; the player reads their width as shelter.
- **The duty-dead,** each still at its old office (the Searcher with a white wand, the Link-boy with a torch, the Spotter pointing, the Mute with a draped wand, the Rattler with plank and mallet): tall thin figures, each with the one long prop that names its duty.
- **Bell-things** (the Tongueless, the Tongue, the Overthrown, Bell-bearers, the Mould-walker): bells on legs, clappers hopping end over end, bells rolling on their wheels; all cones, cylinders and spheres.
- **Ranks** (the Row, Linkmen, the Layer, Jacks): formations of identical figures, read by the formation: one lit place in a row, thin boxes of chain between pairs, a rigid white slab, arms rising together.
- **The drowned** (Drifters, the Belled, the Cered, Salters, Stone-bellies, Wick-walkers): pale spheres bobbing face down, white cylinders riding high on the water, cones on legs, white crusted cubes, a red sphere gliding over something unseen, walking flames.
- **The bound** (the Mortsafe, the Coin-eyed, Dole-seekers, the Bier Party): figures marked by what was done to them at burial: an iron cage of thin boxes, two bright discs for eyes, flat upturned hands, a bier carried by four.
- **The living** (Resurrection-men): pairs with a sack, in warm skin colours among the grey.
- **Forfeits** (the Forfeit Wheel, the Kiddle, the Bucket-chain): things, not bodies; wheels, stake fences, chains of boxes.

## Sound direction

Everything heard is made by code from the engine's recipes (`bell`, `tone`, `noise`, `click`, `creak`, `crackle`, `water`, `note`) and its change-ringing music.

- **The dumb toll.** A struck bell with no ring: a heavy thud and a short inharmonic clank, low-passed, with almost no tail. It is the most important sound in the game and must carry on a phone speaker.
- **The count.** After each toll the Lantern's watch rings the forecast on a thin hand-bell: nine strokes if the next interval brings mostly large dead, six for a mixed crowd, three for small dead in great numbers; after a pause, one quick stroke for each elite that will rise; then one low stroke if a lychgate will open. A setting draws the same count as marks at the screen's edge for play without sound.
- **The true voice.** When a keeper falls the district's bell speaks: a full bell with its five partials (hum, prime, tierce, quint, nominal) and a long decay. Every later toll in that district rings true.
- **Harm.** A Blow is a short knock with a body thump. Drag is a low rasp that loops softly while the player is pressed, louder as it bites harder. A Swell is a rising hum while it fills, then a deep strike when it lands; inside its lee the hum ducks away, so cover can be heard.
- **Posture.** Brace taking hold is a soft wooden click. Stride is wind rising in the ear. Wrong-footed is a short scuff of grit.
- **Wounds.** While wounds lie open a slow low pulse sounds, quickening as the oldest nears its scar. Mending is a tone rising with each tick of life returned. A scar is a dull cracked note.
- **Voices.** Each voice sounds its trade: ringing strokes, raps, the toll-bar's creak, sparks, pipe notes and bellows breath, spadefuls of wet earth.
- **Water.** Wading sloshes, brighter as it deepens; currents hiss; the turn of the tide is a long drag of shingle; a spring tide brings a distant roar.
- **Music.** Each district has its own ringing method in its own scale, built from the engine's change-ringing rows. While the bell is dumb the music is half-muffled, every second row a faint echo of the one before, and its intensity rises with the tolls. When the keeper falls the method opens up. The Lantern plays plain hunt on hand-bells.

## Where the two spines disagreed

- **The game's words:** the hands spine's words were taken (voice, knot, favour, Hum, the Bellframe, Bill, bequest, peal), with calling, observance and Swell added, because sigil, binding, chime and Echoes are stock words of other games (Echoes is another game's currency) and the game's skills should speak.
- **Hub:** the Lantern over the Loft, because the one lit place above the water explains itself at a glance.
- **Districts:** the Dial and the Ropewalk over the Nave and the Close, because a first district must draw the toll on the ground, and the Nave's slosh would be a second tide in a game that already has one.
- **The Strand is renamed the Foreshore:** "strand" is the coined word of a well-known game.
- **The tide in every district (from the depth spine), with each district's ground deciding how much it matters,** because one rule then gives five different districts; the Dial and the Hanging Quarter are dry so that the first and last fights are about the dead, not the water.
- **Keepers:** the Lock-keeper over the Keeper of the Sunk Bell for the Foreshore, because its last phase makes the lee the whole fight; the Conductor is renamed the Ringing Master to keep clear of another game's boss.
